# Our principles for ranking results
Google Search is designed to organize information in a way that is helpful and relevant to those coming to Google looking for information. Every time someone searches, there are hundreds, thousands, or even millions of web pages and other content that might be a match for their query.
Earning and maintaining the trust of those who search with us is essential. This means that we treat all results equally based on their helpfulness for the query. We don't design our ranking systems or AI models to favor any specific site's interests. For more information on our approach, see our Honest Results Policy.
To deliver on this goal, all the parts of our system for ranking and organizing content—from classic ranking systems to deep learning models to generative AI—are guided by three core principles.
## Users come first
Search is designed to serve people looking for information. We prioritize helpful, reliable information that's created to benefit people. By making it easier for people to find the most useful information for their queries, we help them make valuable connections with content creators, businesses, organizations, and others on the open web.
## Our systems are consistent across all types of results to deliver the most helpful results
Since the early days of Search, it has evolved beyond blue links to meet user expectations by including many different types of results such as images, videos, charts, maps, AI experiences, and so on. Our systems are designed to ensure all of these results are upheld to the same standard, namely to their helpfulness to the query.
To deliver the best results:
- All of our systems evaluate and rank information based on utility, which we define as how useful a result is for the query. This means that web page listings and other results like AI Overviews, carousels, images, and videos are evaluated using the same criteria. No result receives preferential treatment. We measure the helpfulness of our results according to the criteria outlined in our Search Quality Rater Guidelines, which are published publicly for anyone to read, and we use the help of independent quality raters to assess how well our systems are working. Learn more about how data from raters is used by Google.
- Similarly, the sources surfaced within AI Overviews and AI Mode are designed to be as helpful as possible for people in the context of their questions.
- We build systems that generalize across billions of queries. Our automated systems determine if a particular result or feature (like a map, a web result, or an AI Overview) makes sense to display, and where on the page it should appear, in terms of usefulness to the user. We don't make manual adjustments to alter search rankings other than to comply with legal obligations or apply our clearly defined Content Policies for Search. When content violates these policies, we take appropriate action.
## We uphold our standards through rigorous testing
User expectations are always evolving, so Search will always need to evolve. To improve Search for users, we continuously experiment and evaluate to test every ranking change. We continually evaluate our ranking systems in the following ways:
- Testing for usefulness: Every change we make to Search is backed by measurements and evaluations on whether they improve utility for users. We evaluate changes by measuring the overall helpfulness of the Search results page, across all different result types. Learn more about rigorous testing on How Search Works.
- Evaluating the quality of results: We use our publicly available Search Quality Rater Guidelines to define and describe our standards for helpful, reliable information. We align our evaluations with these external guidelines so that our goals can be clear, transparent, and publicly accountable.
## Our ongoing commitment
As Search continues to evolve and incorporate new technologies such as sophisticated deep learning models to generative AI, our foundational commitment to users remains unchanged.
These principles, alongside our Honest Results Policy, form the foundation of how we build, test, and refine Google Search. We believe that by maintaining a clear focus on delivering the most relevant, reliable information, we support a web ecosystem where creators can successfully connect with people searching for their content. We are committed to anchoring every change to Search in a review by our most experienced engineers and data scientists, and we maintain a strict standard that if we can't show a change actually makes things better for people, we don't launch it. We will continue to hold our systems to these high standards of transparency and accountability.
