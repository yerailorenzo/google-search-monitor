# How the Google-Extended Product Token Works
<https://developers.google.com/crawling/docs/google-extended>

# Overview of the Google-Extended token
Google-Extended is a standalone product token that enables web publishers to manage whether content crawled by Google is used for training future generations of foundation models that power commercially available Google generative AI products and services which generate creative or informational output, and grounding (providing content from the Google Search index to the model at prompt time to improve factuality and relevancy) in those Google products, aside from Search and other Search services. Google-Extended doesn't apply to training models and grounding used to detect and prevent spam, abuse, security threats, fraud, and other harmful content or behavior, and to protect the safety and security of users and Google services; to specialized models that are trained or fine-tuned to perform discrete, task-specific capabilities, such as robotics models or standalone models that aren't incorporated into a broader multi-modal system, and features powered by them; to accessibility, transcription, and translation features and models; and, to products and features already covered by other consents.
Google-Extended doesn't impact a site's inclusion in Google Search nor is it used as a ranking signal in Google Search.
To manage whether your content is used for grounding in generative AI features on Google Search (such as AI Overviews and AI Mode), use the Search generative AI control in Google Search Console.
## How Google-Extended works
In order to maximize efficiency and help websites save bandwidth, Google doesn't run a separate "Google-Extended web crawler". Instead, when Googlebot, GoogleOther, or other training- and grounding-related crawlers crawl a web page, they record the page's Google-Extended status. Google's systems then apply the status to restrict downstream training and non-Search grounding for pages and sites that have disallowed Google-Extended.
Each website owner is responsible for applying robots.txt controls on the domains where their content is published or syndicated. Like all robots.txt directives, the Google-Extended token lives in the robots.txt file at the root of your site, and can be applied to your entire site or specific paths. Google-Extended doesn't prevent the same or similar content from other websites or pages (that haven't disallowed Google-Extended) from being used in grounding or training.
Models may still output references or links to domains that have disallowed Google-Extended. Even if your website disallows Google-Extended, if other websites (that haven't disallowed Google-Extended) mention or link to your site, then your site might be mentioned or linked to in generative AI responses as a result.
Google intends to support the shared machine-readable training preference signal (with user-agent: __usage_ai-training) in early 2027.
